# Talking-head Clips

Start from the source audio and a timestamped transcript when available. Verify names, numbers, technical terms, and quote boundaries against the recording. Do not claim to have transcribed or listened when only text is available; identify the available transcription/playback tools before relying on them.

Choose a self-contained thought with a clear beginning and conclusion. Remove tangents and excessive pauses while preserving qualifiers, negation, attribution, and the speaker’s intended meaning. Do not splice separated phrases into a claim the speaker did not make. Keep breaths and pauses that make delivery natural.

Use J/L cuts, relevant B-roll, or a modest reframing to cover visual discontinuities. Supporting footage should illustrate what is actually being said, not imply unsupported evidence. Check eye lines, lip synchronization, jump cuts and abrupt room-tone changes.

Create speech captions from verified words. Segment by phrases and reading rhythm; avoid excessive word-by-word bouncing. Place captions clear of faces and important visuals. Adjust line length, dwell time and contrast for the requested format. Export an SRT when requested or useful, using actual timings; do not invent word-level alignment from a paragraph transcript.

Prioritize intelligible speech. Use gentle cleanup and level control; strong denoising can damage consonants. Preserve a little room tone across cuts, duck music under speech, and review plosives, sibilance and clipped peaks with the audio skill.

Handoff: final clip, editable project, caption file when applicable, and source ranges. Check the edit against the original meaning, caption accuracy and synchronization before delivery.

Use [Blender Video](../blender-vse-video/SKILL.md) for version-aware execution, [Audio & Sound Design](../blender-vse-audio-sound-design/SKILL.md) when mixing, and [Video Review & Delivery](../blender-vse-video-review-delivery/SKILL.md) before handoff. Preserve the brief’s duration, style, format and source constraints; these are editorial guidance, not a fixed template. Follow source inspection → plan → scripted timeline → preview → inspection → revision → final render.
