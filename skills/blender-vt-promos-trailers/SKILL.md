# Promos & Trailers

Identify the audience, supported promise, strongest evidence, and requested call to action. Respect supplied brand rules, dates and factual claims. Do not invent testimonials, availability, results, discounts or urgency.

Choose an opening that creates interest through a concrete image, question, result or contrast. Develop anticipation by revealing selected evidence. A teaser can withhold detail; an advertisement still needs enough clarity to understand the offer. Use an escalation and a deliberate ending rather than a sequence of interchangeable flashy shots.

Cut visual changes and sound accents together where it helps. Alternate intensity with a pause or hold so the payoff has contrast. Typography should carry a small number of useful claims. Check phone readability and avoid covering the strongest image with a giant permanent title.

Use color, type, motion and sound as a coherent system. Do not imply affiliation or endorsement through unprovided logos or mock testimonials. Use only assets available for the intended output; keep licensing or provenance uncertainty visible when relevant rather than claiming clearance.

Reserve enough ending time for the requested action, destination and necessary date/details. A teaser need not include a sales CTA if the brief asks only for mood or awareness. Review whether a first-time viewer can state what is being promoted and what to do next.

Handoff: final render, editable project, approved/supplied claims and asset references, and concise creative decisions. Do not publish the video or launch an advertising campaign without explicit authorization.

Use [Blender Video](../blender-vt-video/SKILL.md) for version-aware execution, [Audio & Sound Design](../blender-vt-audio-sound-design/SKILL.md) when mixing, and [Video Review & Delivery](../blender-vt-video-review-delivery/SKILL.md) before handoff. Preserve the brief’s duration, style, format and source constraints; these are editorial guidance, not a fixed template. Follow source inspection → plan → scripted timeline → preview → inspection → revision → final render.
